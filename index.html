Open Keepsake

The memory card

Seven little steps, one memory card.

Pick a colour, say or type the story, choose a texture, pin the place, name the sense, choose how it felt. Kip weaves it all into a card, with a polaroid, a taped corner, and your own handwriting. Go back and change anything.

Photos, videos, places

Add a photo or a video. Pin the place.

Type where you were and Kip drops a pin on the map. Or zoom in and tap the exact spot. Add pictures and short videos, and they sit right on the card, in the place they happened.

Music and sound

Soft jazz, and a soundtrack for every memory.

A little jazz plays while you keep things, with tiny sounds as you turn each page. Add a song to a memory like you would on a story: a title, an artist, and a link out to Spotify or YouTube Music. Memories can also play what they sound like. Tap one to hear it.

Sounds are made live in your browser. Turn your volume up a little. In the app, the music starts on your first tap.

The shaker keychain

Shake a memory loose.

Tiny polaroids and stars rattle around inside a keychain charm. In the app you shake your phone, drag the charm or tap the button, and one of your memories falls out. Try it here.

The memory box

A little pine box for the keepers.

Flip the brass latch and the box opens toward you, onto a scrapbook calendar. Stick stickers where you like, move them around, take them off again. It is yours to fill.

The memory reel

Spin it. Stop on a page. Open it.

Every memory lands on a wheel of numbered polaroids. Spin it, let it blur, and it settles on a picture. The pages are numbered in the order you kept them.

Seven senses

What does it … like?

Every memory starts with one of seven little sentences. Tap one to read it.

Meet Kip

A snail who carries your memories.

Kip says hello when you open the app, asks how you are and why you’re here, and sits at the bottom of every page telling you what to do next. He leaves a dotted trail wherever he goes. Give him a tap.

Hi! I’m Kip. I carry little memories on my back, the ones words can’t hold.
